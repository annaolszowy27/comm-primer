Umberto Eco, a philosopher studying concepts called intentio lectoris which then connects with intentio algorithmi, laid out how important the receiver’s role is when understanding or interpreting a message being sent. He described how interpretations of messages are determined because of culture, experiences, wants, beliefs, and more.


Intention lectoris sets a foundation for intentio algorithmi in the sense that algorithms must try and understand words, images, or objects. The question this creates for people to think about is how much meaning the message holds versus how much power does is given to the receiver to interpret it. 

Intentio algorithmi is necessary in today’s AI era. This model states the algorithm is a key step, not simply a tool in the process. This means even more now with the rise of artificial intelligence in areas such as customer service representation. AI is pulling from previous knowledge it’s been fed, matching patterns it’s recognizing.

While applying Eco’s framework as well as the extension of this model, it can be used to distinguish what was originally meant in a message and then what was truly interpreted from the reader. Intentio algorithmi can be for when one is understanding why they are being shown something online, or when AI is responding to them in a certain way.

However, these frameworks provide curiosities that can could challenge this way of thinking such as if AI has any intention, since it doesn’t feel any emotions and is more of an input, output kind of software. Can AI interpret a message or is that only up to humans and their own backgrounds or experiences?

---
title: The transmission view and its limits
lead: "What Shannon-Weaver illuminates, and what humanist critique reveals that the mathematical model misses."
permalink: /pages/transmission-view/
---

The transmission view treats communication as the movement of information from sender to receiver through a channel. It is useful because it clarifies structure: source, encoding, message, noise, decoding, and reception.

In modern media systems, this lens helps readers ask practical questions: Where does distortion enter? What signals are amplified? What is filtered out?

At the same time, human communication is not only signal transfer. Humanist critiques remind us that meaning, relationship, and context cannot be reduced to throughput. The model measures efficiency well, but it cannot fully explain ambiguity, emotion, trust, or social consequences.

Use this lens to map technical flow, then pair it with interpretive frameworks when human stakes are central.

Semiotics, the study of symbols and how we get their meanings, affect how communication flows. The kind of meaning behind the signifier is what helps the signified be formed. Semiotics can include images, gestures, or sounds which all rely on some sort of cultural background. Understandings of these symbols can vary depending on someone’s origin or location. 

In communication, messages can be drastically changed if someone “misinterprets” what the sender meant initially. It is not what the symbol literally means but how it is understood. 

Entering an image into AI is allowing it to interpret what it is seeing solely based on what the software has learned already. It has no cultural background, just what others have entered as a prompt previously. AI is assuming what the sender meant and what it wants to learn.

However, there are cases in today’s technology such as emoticons that can hold different meanings based on who is sending them. For example, a Gen X user might use the crying emoji face (😭) as a laughing reaction, while AI interprets the face as sad or literally crying.

This model proves that meanings or symbols aren’t universal. Everyone carries different meanings for different symbols. This concept can be used when deciphering semiotics and analyzing what an image means in media with context.
